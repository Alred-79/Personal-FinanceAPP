## 1. Vision & thesis

**The game is a lens over real money — never a second reality.** Every number the game shows is a
real number wearing a costume, and the costume comes off with one tap.

**The corrected hierarchy (the meaningfulness audit's one-sentence verdict):** across 15+ years of
shipped fintech, the only mechanisms with *causal* money-outcome evidence are —

1. **Information arriving at the action moment with a low-friction fix attached** (auto-enrolled
   alerts: −4 to −24% overdraft charges in Journal-of-Finance/FCA RCTs `[T1]`),
2. **Guaranteed/scheduled transfers** (CFPB-verified $167.84/mo vs $80.36 for round-ups `[T1-C]`),
3. **Passive transaction/balance visibility** (−11.6% discretionary spend, persistent 12 months
   `[T1]`).

Everything else — narrators, maps, bosses, chapters, streaks — is, on the evidence, a **delivery
vehicle and retention engine for those three**. v4 embraces this instead of resenting it: the
**Money Spine (§5)** is the product; the **game (§6–§9) is what makes people stay close enough to
the spine to feel it work**, and what makes this app impossible to confuse with anything else.
That division of labor is the design's honesty and its strategy.

**The two edges, confirmed by the uniqueness audit:** the market splits into two non-overlapping
camps — gamified trackers with no real data (Hunter Vault, Fortune City, Habitica, Toshl) and
data-rich AI finance apps with no game (Monarch, Copilot, Era, Piere, Bright, YNAB). **No shipped
product runs game mechanics on a real cashflow forecast with bank-verified state.** That seam —
*the forecast is the world generator and the bank feed is the truth source* — requires the boring
infrastructure the game-first indies don't have and the data-first incumbents won't risk their
brand on. The second edge is unchanged: ethics as strategy (XP only on verified good financial
actions; deterministic rewards stated publicly — a differentiator as the variable-reward fintechs
drift gambling-ward `[Market]`).

**North star (unchanged):** *did the user act on a hard day*, and did anxiety/avoidance fall —
measured against an assigned holdout, on money outcomes. Engagement is diagnostic, never the goal.
New in v4 (R12): the holdout results are eventually **published** — audited outcomes as product.

**Honesty clause:** the gamified-finance evidence base is one N=331, 4-week RCT plus pilots.
Realistic effect sizes are single-digit percents and hundreds of dollars a year. v4's claim is not
"gamification fixes finances"; it is "evidence-backed mechanics, delivered through a world users
want to return to, beat the same mechanics in a dashboard nobody opens."

## 3. The Three Laws (architecture & ethics — non-negotiable)

### Law 1 — The fold. `game_state = fold(event_stream)`
A deterministic projection over an append-only event log that **never writes to financial truth**.
Refined by R8: **bank truth stays in the Plaid-synced CRUD tables** (the existing
`transaction_service`/`plaid_service` world); the event log is the source of truth for the *game
domain only*, with finance-derived events emitted from sync diffs under the §12.3 rules. Pure stat
engine in `server/src/services/game/`, replayable; projections rebuild from the log with a
one-command tool that exists from day one (§12.5). Game tables are reconstructable caches.

### Law 2 — Two layers. The deterministic engine is authority; the LLM is flavor and judgment, never arithmetic and never state.
The engine computes every number, awards every XP, resolves every boss hit. The LLM (existing
`assistant_service.py`: LangChain over Groq, Tavily, hardened fetch, propose→confirm with signed
one-use tokens) narrates, names, taunts, compiles rules, and renders verdicts whose consequences
are computed by code. **No LLM output ever feeds engine state.** Generation on cadence, cached
(content-hash keys, §12.6), regenerable.

### Law 3 — The charter, hardened.
Governing test: *does this improve the user's actual financial health, or just our engagement
metrics?* Hard lines (additions from the audits in bold):

- **XP hygiene:** XP only on verified financial actions; never on opens/checks/taps/logging.
- **XP never decreases** — corrections clamp at 0 (R8); true deltas are stored for audit, financial
  displays always show truth.
- **Never juice an almost**; celebration on confirmed outcomes only (the Robinhood rule).
- **No variable-ratio rewards on money actions** — deterministic rewards, stated publicly.
- **No fictional money, ever** — and per R2, no trivial-sum real-money theater either ("you earned
  $0.43") — accrual surfaces only as forward projection + action.
- **Data errors resolve pro-player**; sync outages = fog-of-war, never failure.
- **Setbacks are soft**; numbers stay legible; every game element reveals its real number (the
  dual-label *is* the reveal).
- **Alerts/notifications never nudge a transaction or purchase**; frequency caps are immutable —
  and per R4, the alerts that *do* fire are the evidence-backed kind: action-moment, one-tap-fix,
  default-on with easy off.
- **Timeless monetization lines** (avenue deferred, constraints permanent): no paid streak repair,
  no purchasable randomness, no purchasable currency, nothing payment-accelerable.
- **AI hygiene:** Chronicler persistently labeled AI; crisis-routing for distress signals; no
  allocation/product/borrowing advice (classifier-enforced); **no outcome claims that aren't
  holdout-measured** (the Cleo-FTC and Bright-Money lesson — unverifiable "$X saved" claims are
  both dishonest and now demonstrably fineable).

## 5. The Money Spine (the evidence-first core — new top-level system, R4)

Everything in this section has causal money-outcome evidence. The game exists to keep users close
to these four surfaces. **If v4 were cut to one section, it would be this one.**

### 5.1 Action-moment alerts with a one-tap fix
- **Design:** default-on (with easy off), capped, and *just-in-time* — fired when the forecast +
  balance state says the decision moment is now ("Rent clears in 2 days; checking will dip to
  −$48 — move $60 from savings?"), each with exactly one tap to fix (a propose→confirm action).
  **Not** early predictive warnings shown days ahead as passive info — the FCA's >1M-user
  experiment found those underperform `[T1]`; the same forecast fact should *arm* an alert that
  fires at the action window, not decorate a dashboard a week early.
- **Why default-on matters:** voluntary alert uptake was 3–10% in the UK RCTs; auto-enrollment was
  the difference between an effect and a rounding error `[T1]`.
- **Game integration:** the alert *is* a map event (the encounter "approaches"); fixing it is the
  parry. The skin changes with intensity mode; the alert logic doesn't.
- **Metric:** overdraft/late-fee incidence vs. holdout (the literature's own DV), alert→action
  rate, opt-out rate (a rising opt-out is a design failure, not a user failure).

### 5.2 The glanceable number (R13)
One Safe-to-Spend figure — computed by v1's engine, rendered identically in all three modes,
designed for the smallest surfaces first (browser tab title, PWA badge/widget, lock-screen when
native). Passive visibility is the cheapest causal effect in the literature (−5 to −11.6%
discretionary `[T1]`); Even's number was checked 4 days/week `[Market]`. The number is *the* app
icon of the product's value. **Avoid:** surrounding it with secondary numbers (the TFI
budget-licensing caution `[T1-C]` — remaining-budget HUDs invite spending to the line; E4 tests
presentation).

### 5.3 Standing-transfer graduation (the challenge → rule pipeline)
Every challenge, Heat contract, and what-if commit has one designed *success exit*: **becoming a
standing rule** (v1 engine; the CFPB's guaranteed-transfer class, 2× round-ups `[T1-C]`). "You
held dining 40% under baseline for 3 weeks — make the $30/week sweep permanent?" And one designed
*failure exit*: a soft landing that never leaves the user below baseline (StepBet's losers ended
−5.3% *below* their own baseline `[T1-C]` — the anti-pattern; a failed contract here ends with
"the realm holds; nothing is lost" and a smaller re-offer).

### 5.4 What-if with commit (R14)
The simulator's displayed assumptions are themselves the intervention `[T1]`: default to a
*reachable, slightly-better* scenario; always terminate in one-tap commit (rule or transfer);
never render a hopeless trajectory unprompted; strain-mode (Grace) suppresses long-horizon
projections entirely (68% of low-balance users react negatively to bleak numbers `[T2]`).

### 5.5 Published outcomes (R12)
The assigned holdout (§13) eventually feeds a public, plainly-worded outcomes page ("members paid
bills on time X% more often than the holdout"). No competitor does this; the FCA is building a
regime because nobody does. It is the only marketing claim in fintech that compounds.

## 6. Engine substrate

### 6.1 Event log + stat engine
As first drafted, with R8's corrections model (full blueprint §12). Stats are **ratios vs. the
user's own 3-month baselines**, never absolutes (anti-wealth-ranking, anti-shame, and the only
fair Heat input). Character sheet = output surface; ambient header pip in Ambient/Full.
Essential categories (groceries, rent, medical, insurance, childcare) are structurally protected:
never quest targets, never monster seeds, never Armor penalties.

### 6.2 Grace (uniqueness 4 — flagship-adjacent)
Deterministic hard-month detection (income drop vs. baseline, essential-category shock,
`will_go_negative`, Safe-to-Spend floor) → quest targets scale down, defeat-by-dates re-derive
(boss "retreats"), a ward auto-equips, the daily beat enters strain mode (one number + one action,
behavioral-moat F3), long-horizon projections suppressed (R14). Capped, reversible, opt-out in
settings, never names the trigger in game copy. The Garmin precedent says users accept silent
deterministic easing when it's consistently right `[Market]` — which is why the trigger is
deterministic, not vibes.

### 6.3 Streaks: policy, not marquee (R10)
Definition: consecutive days in the green (under discretionary budget / Safe-to-Spend positive).
Wards: 2 visible slots, auto-applied, post-hoc notice, replenished only by good behavior. Display
contract (D8): a broken streak renders as "rebuilding — day 1 (best: 23)"; restart credits day 1
immediately. **Revival Run** (new): a dead streak ≥30 days can be revived once per quarter via 3
consecutive green days — re-earning beats mourning, and it converts the lapse into a quest.
**Avoid:** any streak surface in Off mode beyond a quiet consistency stat; any copy implying a
streak "died."

### 6.4 The Homestead (idle, redesigned — R2)
- **Daily clock:** overnight tick → small capped XP from yesterday's verified state; "collect" is
  a micro-moment, not a number theater.
- **Weekly clock:** the reveal — hero content is the **forward projection with one-tap action**
  ("the orchard's pace: $1,240/yr — graft another branch? +$25/mo"), with real accrued
  interest/sweeps as the *supporting* line, only when non-trivial. Tickers of cents are cut.
- **Monthly/quarterly:** compounding visualizer on real balances; milestone XP multipliers at real
  thresholds (1 month of runway, etc.).
- **Ascendancy (prestige):** end-of-expedition scoring → sub-linear prestige from real positive
  deltas; buys variety only (modifiers, biomes, personas, cosmetic ranks); resets only game-layer.

### 6.5 Heat, self-funding (R5 — the bridge between game and spine)
The user composes constraint contracts priced in Heat against their own baselines; **the payoff is
real**: verified underspend vs. baseline can sweep (opt-in rule, propose→confirm, forecast-vetoed)
into the active boss or a goal — *the underspend is the boss damage*. Cosmetic/prestige rewards
ride on top. Caps per month; essentials excluded; abandoning costs only the bonus; sweeps pause
automatically under Grace. Judged only against user-confirmed category mappings; ambiguity
pro-player. **This is the loop nothing on the market closes:** Qapital has the sweep without the
game; Hunter Vault has the game without the money; Forfeit has stakes without bank truth.

## 7. Flagship modules

### 7.1 Debt Boss Battles (closure-framed — D2, R11)
One account = one boss (concentration); payments posted via Plaid = damage; above-minimum = crit;
APR accrual = boss heal; missed real bill = boss *empowered* (agency points at the debt, never the
user). **Defeat-by-date** from `project_cashflows`, live-updating — the line nobody else can
render honestly. **Victory = the closure event**, with the seal-vs-banish informed choice (close
the account vs. keep at zero; static educational copy on credit-score effects; the Chronicler
stays silent on the choice — Law 3). Multi-phase HP for large debts (each 20% = a phase break +
lore drop). Smallest-first default with the one-time informed override (user's own APR delta in
dollars). Round-up + Heat-sweep auto-attack via the v1 generic-target rule. Habit-monsters from
discretionary overruns + subscriptions (cancellation proves itself by the first *absent* expected
charge — copy sets that expectation). Regret-monster per R9.

**Marketing rule (R11):** the public story is *"your real payments, verified by your bank, with a
defeat date computed from your actual cashflow — and the win is the day the account closes."* The
boss metaphor is the skin on that sentence, not the sentence.

### 7.2 Expeditions (the month-map — D3)
Generated per §5.2-of-the-forecast (acts = high-confidence income events, else weekly skeleton;
bills = fixed nodes; guaranteed rest windows before the largest obligations; optional forks only
where the forecast shows slack; skipping always free). Draft of 1-of-3 *interacting* modifiers;
elite weekly routes; random events = real surprise expenses reskinned (and simultaneously Grace
checks); **pending transactions render as fog — "the encounter hasn't settled"** (R8's
engineering constraint turned into a mechanic). End-of-run scoring with **no month-end timing
bonuses** (TFI distortion `[T1-C]`); failure mints story (counter-built modifiers + antagonist
lore); Ascension opt-in. The map is Full-mode's home (§10); in Off mode the same data is the
Forecast page — same component, different tokens and terms.

**Honesty (scorecard):** the map is RETENTION; the *alerts that ride its nodes* are the MONEY. A
gorgeous passive calendar that doesn't fire §5.1 beats replicates the FCA's null. Every map node
that matters must be able to become an action-moment alert.

### 7.3 Campaigns (quarterly — D14)
Three expeditions, one themed antagonist arc, milestone track: non-expiring, retroactive
catch-up, zero countdown timers. The long arc (rank ascension, the Seven Lords, net-worth saga)
lives here, fed by prestige and bestiary — no new systems.

### 7.4 The Chronicler (overlay, not place — R6, D11)
A command-palette/REPL drawer (`/`) over any screen — native to the terminal fiction, doubles as
search/assistant in Off mode, removes a nav tab. Persona law: **antagonist-coach** — roasts the
monsters, respects the Keeper; never needy, never disappointed-in-you; gentle register default,
roast opt-in. Human-authored voice bible (~50 exemplars — Cleo's comedians are the bar we route
around, not through: we out-*world*, not out-roast). Canon file: engine-written fact store (~500
tokens injected per generation). Advice firewall: output classifier + in-character redirect +
red-team CI. Speaks on cadence beats + rate-limited summon.

## 8. The generative layer (cadence jobs)

### 8.1 Weekly Saga Chapter — build first (D10)
Deterministic fact sheet → 150–250-word chapter in voice → chapter card with deterministic
archetype title. **R-rule (from the recap evidence):** every chapter terminates in exactly **one
tappable action** (this week's single highest-value move, engine-chosen) — a recap that is only a

### 8.2 Bestiary (generate-once, cache-forever)
Engine detects the pattern and computes all rules; LLM fills a strict JSON schema once; seeded by
`hash(merchant_id | subscription_id)` so the same bill is always the same monster — **recognition
is the feature** `[Eng]`. Defeated bosses/monsters collect permanently (Fortune City's 4-year
collectors are the retention precedent `[Market]`).

### 8.3 Monthly Expedition Trailer
Five lines over the deterministic bill calendar. One generation/user/month.

### 8.4 Daily beat
6/7 days template (human-authored), 1/7 LLM-personalized; strain-mode set under Grace. One line,
one optional verb (§9.1), never more.

### 8.5 Spells = the rules engine's skin (R1)
NL → closed-grammar rule compilation (trigger/condition/action enums), preview-as-contract,
propose→confirm, then deterministic forever. **Positioned as infrastructure** — it's the §5.3
graduation rail and the Heat-sweep/auto-attack mechanism. Era ships this pipeline; our delta is
that spells feed a game economy with real stakes on real data.

### 8.6 Challenges (tiered evidence)
Tier 1 (full XP): transaction-data-verifiable, judged by code. Tier 2 (reduced XP): web-grounded,
reference-anchored LLM verdict, engine awards. Tier 3 (cosmetic): photo/self-report — cheating is
priced in by keeping stakes cosmetic. Every challenge has the §5.3 graduation and soft-landing
exits.

### 8.7 Failure modes (ranked) & cost
Unchanged from first draft in substance: novelty cliff (mitigated by deterministic progression +
weekly appointment + §9.4), advice leakage (firewall), canon incoherence (validators), sycophancy
(human few-shots; code-computed verdicts), jailbreak (inconsequential by Law 2), cost
(cadence-only: ~$0.004–0.05/user/month at Groq-class pricing; the only dangerous surface —
unmetered chat — is rate-limited by design), anti-AI sentiment (market the character, not the
generator). LLM cache: content-hash keyed, permanent for sealed months (§12.6).

## 9. Retention, activation, and the behavioral spine

### 9.1 The daily verb (D12)
At most **one verb + one collect per day**, drawn from: confirm the forecast ("the map holds?" —
corrections feed `recurring_service` accuracy), scout the next bill, one what-if (R14 rules), or

### 9.2 Onboarding, value-first (R3 — rewritten)
Sequence: (1) **one real insight before any wall** — link via Plaid (read-only promise stated
plainly: "we can see, never touch") and immediately render the user's real Safe-to-Spend number
and their month-map preview; (2) signup/profile deferred to the latest possible moment; (3) the
first XP comes from a real act (first under-budget day closing, first allocation); (4) the
character sheet is *generated and presented* as an output ("here is what the Ledger sees") — never
a questionnaire. A 3-question quiz is permissible only if every answer visibly changes the plan on
screen. **Cut:** summon-your-power character creation, tutorial-boss-before-value, any screen

### 9.3 Temptation-bundled check-in (E3)
Unchanged: the day's fun content dispenses only bundled with the 60-second exposure to the

### 9.4 Surviving the trough (D15)
Campaign milestone, new draft-pool drop, and first phase-break scheduled into weeks 4–8;

### 9.5 The behavioral six (from behavioral-moat — unchanged mapping)

## 10. Holistic frontend restructure (IA, modes, and the one-variable law)

### 10.1 Two homes, one skeleton (R7)

The audits' clearest IA finding: daily-habit products want a **today-loop home** (Duolingo's
path, Finch's single-screen loop), finance tools want a **dense actionable dashboard** (Monarch,
Copilot). A dual-mode app needs both — and only the home differs; everything below it is one
component tree re-skinned.

| Nav slot | OFF (pure tool) | AMBIENT | FULL RPG |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Home | **Overview** — dense actionable dashboard: Safe-to-Spend hero, upcoming bills, alerts, net worth | Overview + quiet streak/level chips | **Today / The Expedition** — the month-map path: next encounter, boss HP, the daily verb, collect |
| 2 Money | Transactions | Transactions (+fog states) | **The Ledger** (same table, terminal skin) |
| 3 Plan | Budgets · Goals · Subscriptions (grouped) | same + progress framing | **Quests** (sustain-quests · vaults · recurring encounters) |
| 4 Future | Forecast | Forecast + expedition preview | **The Map** (same data, run-rendered) |
| 5 Accounts | Accounts | Accounts | **Vaults & Holdings** |
| Overflow | Settings | Settings | **Keeper sheet** (character/bestiary/homestead/campaign) + Settings |
| Overlay | Assistant/search drawer (`/`) | same | **The Chronicler** (same drawer, persona on) |

Rules: **degradation is monotone** (Full→Ambient removes map-as-home + renames nothing else;
Ambient→Off removes chips + switches the term catalog); **no page exists in only one mode** —
that's the fork-smell test; the boring data is never hidden, only re-labeled (Hunter Vault's
proof; Habitica's failure was chrome obstructing data `[Market]`); existing pages
(`client/src/pages/{Overview,Accounts,Transactions,Budgets,Goals,Subscriptions,Forecast,Settings}`)
are *re-homed into this skeleton*, not duplicated.

### 10.2 Mode is one variable, end to end

`mode ∈ {off, ambient, full}` drives, and *only* this variable drives:

1. **Which home route renders** (the only true component swap).
2. **Design tokens:** `data-mode` attribute on `<html>` switching semantic CSS custom properties
   (`--bg-surface`, `--accent`, `--crt-intensity: 0 | .4 | 1`, `--radius-panel`) under Tailwind's
   theme — runtime switch, no rebuild, components never branch on mode `[Eng]`.
3. **The term catalog:** copy as an i18n-style dictionary — three "locales" of the same keys
   (`budget.title` → "Budgets" / "Budgets · streak 4" / "Mana Pools"), a `useTerm()` hook reading
   `terms.{off,ambient,full}.json`. Dual-labeling formalized; the plain term is the reveal.
4. **Feature-presence flags** (XP bar, narrator persona, map elements) — presence, not behavior.
5. **Server-side generation:** mode persists on the `User` row; the backend skips narrator/flavor
   generation entirely for Off-mode users (cost + honesty).

If a feature needs more than this variable, it is a fork smell and gets redesigned. The toggle
itself is the unshipped differentiator (§2.2) — treat its crispness as a flagship feature.

### 10.3 The Chronicler drawer
A `/`-summoned command-palette/REPL over any screen (Revolut-AIR pattern, terminal-native):
query, navigate, propose actions; persona and flavor gated by mode; rate-limited; never a tab.

## 11. The visual system (terminal-RPG, with the taste line drawn)

**Honest classification first (scorecard):** the aesthetic is a brand/acquisition bet, not a
behavior lever — but first-impression trust is real and finance punishes visual sloppiness
hardest `[T2]`. So: distinctive *and* disciplined.

- **Grid discipline before any effect.** Monospace character-grid rigor is what separates
  engineered from costumed (SRCL/sacred.computer is the reference implementation — line-height as
  the vertical unit, `ch`-based widths) `[Eng]`. Spline Sans Mono + tabular numerals + a 4-tone
  phosphor palette are the foundation; invest there first.
- **Color as strict information:** phosphor green = real money truth; amber = game layer; boss
  red reserved for antagonists, never for the user's own numbers. Numbers stay effect-free,
  tabular, scannable in all modes.
- **CRT as one tunable overlay:** static scanlines via `repeating-linear-gradient` (nearly free);
  `--crt-intensity` custom property wired to mode (0 / .4 / 1); animated flicker/glitch only
  behind `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` *and* Full mode; WebGL bloom reserved for the
  map canvas only, never full-page `[Eng]`. Balatro's lesson: the effect carries meaning and
  every layer has an off switch `[Craft]`.
- **Seeded, deterministic generation everywhere:** rot.js (seeded RNG + map generation) for the
  expedition path; mirrored glyph-grid creatures (pixel-sprite-generator algorithm, chars for
  pixels) seeded by merchant/subscription hash — the same bill is always the same monster;
  play.core/textmode.js-style per-cell ASCII animation for ambient texture (fog, idle shimmer) —
  production-proven (Oxide ships brand assets with it) `[Eng]`. Randomized decoration that
  changes between visits is on the gimmick checklist — everything is seeded.
- **Juice doctrine:** Balatro-grade feedback on payoff events only (damage numbers, closure
  celebrations, phase breaks, the weekly reveal) — typography-as-mechanics, threshold
  celebrations; never on transactions, never on almosts (Law 3).
- **Gimmick checklist (auto-reject):** full-page flicker loops; glow on body text; effects
  ignoring reduced-motion; non-seeded decoration; game chrome occluding data.
- **Chapter/victory cards:** 1200×630, archetype title large, boss tier glyph, zero dollars by
  default — the distribution surface; P0 quality within the saga feature.

## 12. Backend restructure (the blueprint — R8)

### 12.1 The architectural split
**Event-sourcing-lite, game-domain only.** Bank truth stays in the existing CRUD tables
(`transactions`, `accounts`, budgets, goals — `plaid_service`/`transaction_service` unchanged in
role); the append-only `events` table is authoritative for the game domain; finance-derived game
events are emitted from sync diffs in the same DB transaction as the CRUD apply (the outbox
guarantee for free) `[Eng]`. Projections fold **synchronously, same-transaction** — no eventual
consistency, no CQRS plumbing; every event-sourcing retrospective says full ES over mutable
upstream data is the trap `[Eng]`.

### 12.2 Schema (Alembic; SQLite now, Postgres-portable)

```sql
CREATE TABLE events (
  id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY,                  -- rowid = global order (single writer)
  stream TEXT NOT NULL,                    -- 'player' | 'month:2026-06' | 'boss:<liability_id>'
  seq INTEGER NOT NULL,                    -- per-stream sequence
  type TEXT NOT NULL,                      -- 'PaymentPosted.v1', 'XpAwarded.v1', 'TxnCorrected.v1'
  v INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 1,
  payload TEXT NOT NULL,                   -- JSON
  idempotency_key TEXT UNIQUE,             -- 'plaid:<txn_id>:posted', 'daily:2026-06-11:tick'
  caused_by INTEGER REFERENCES events(id), -- correction/compensation chains
  created_at TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT (datetime('now')),
  UNIQUE(stream, seq)
);
CREATE TABLE projection_meta (name TEXT PRIMARY KEY, last_event_id INTEGER, rebuilt_at TEXT, status TEXT);
-- projections (rebuildable): player_state, boss_state, run_state, campaign_state, streak_state, heat_state
CREATE TABLE llm_cache (
  cache_key TEXT PRIMARY KEY,              -- sha256(model | template_ver | input_hash | mode)
  kind TEXT NOT NULL, content TEXT NOT NULL, model TEXT NOT NULL,
  created_at TEXT NOT NULL, expires_at TEXT  -- NULL for sealed-month content
);
```

New/changed fields on existing tables: `plaid_items.sync_cursor`; soft-delete on transactions
(`deleted_at`) + `pending_transaction_id` retained; `users.game_mode` (`off|ambient|full`),
`users.persona_prefs`; optional `transactions.worth_it` (one-tap reaction, nullable);
`categories.essential` flag (the §6.1 protection list); the v4 `liabilities` table (the one
financial-truth-adjacent model). v1 enablers reused as planned (`Rule`/`RuleExecution`, generic
rule target, `birth_year` dormant).

### 12.3 The sync-fold rules (Plaid mutability → immutable log)
- **Pending never enters the log.** Plaid surfaces pending→posted as remove+re-add; pending
  renders as **fog** on the map (a UI state). XP and boss damage emit only on `posted`
  (`idempotency_key = 'plaid:<txn_id>:posted'`). This one decision deletes most correction
  complexity `[Eng]`.
- **`modified`** after a game award → append `TxnCorrected.v1 {caused_by, delta}`; folds consume
  deltas, never rewrite.
- **`removed`** → append `TxnReversed.v1 {caused_by}` compensating exactly the original's effects
  (computed from the original event, not current state).
- **The clamp rule (Law 3):** corrections re-derive financial-derived game state (boss HP, map)
  truthfully, but **XP deltas clamp at 0** — store the true delta in the payload, apply the
  clamped one. No clawbacks, no Habitica-style punishment loop `[T1-C]`.
- **Sync gaps:** `SyncGapOpened/Closed` events; fog-of-war pauses streak/quest judgment; the gap
  period is judged leniently on close (wards don't burn in fog).

### 12.4 Event versioning & the month seal
Additive-only payload changes; breaking changes get a new type (`.v2`) + a tiny upcaster registry;
stored events are never migrated `[Eng]`. **`MonthSealed.v1`** at expedition close carries the
folded month summary — one mechanism, four jobs: schema aging (new folds never read old months'
raw events), fast rebuilds (snapshot boundary), a permanent-cache key for the month's narration,
and the "expedition complete" beat itself.

### 12.5 Migration path (strangler, no dual-write)
1. Migration 1: `events`, `projection_meta`, `llm_cache`, new columns.
2. **Backfill = one `BackfillSnapshot.v1` genesis event per stream** capturing current derived
   state — never fabricate synthetic per-transaction history (the audit-fidelity trap) `[Eng]`.
3. Single write path: the Plaid apply-loop updates CRUD rows *and* appends derived events in one
   transaction; game writes go through one `record_events(session, [...])` helper (embrace
   SQLite's single writer — it deletes a class of concurrency bugs).
4. **`python -m app.rebuild <projection>` exists from day one** — truncate, replay by `id`,
   update watermark. The first fold bug becomes a 2-minute rebuild, not a hand-written repair.

### 12.6 Ops
`PRAGMA journal_mode=WAL; synchronous=NORMAL; busy_timeout=5000; foreign_keys=ON`. **WAL is
incompatible with network filesystems: the runtime DB and its `-wal`/`-shm` files live only on
the local tree — never the OneDrive copy** (this repo's dual-tree layout makes this a real
foot-gun). Graduate to Postgres only when a second writer process, multi-user, or network storage
arrives; the schema ports 1:1.

### 12.7 Top pitfalls (ranked, from production retrospectives `[Eng]`)
1. Folding mutable bank data as immutable events (solved by §12.3).
2. Missing idempotency on emission → double XP on replays (the UNIQUE key is non-negotiable).
3. Projection rebuild as an afterthought (solved by §12.5.4).
4. Unversioned payloads / fabricated backfill history (solved by §12.4–12.5).
5. Scope creep into full event sourcing (async projections, CQRS, events-as-truth-for-everything)
   — same-transaction folds in one DB avoid all of it.

## 15. Scoping & build order

**Sequencing truth:** v1 ships first (forecast, rules engine, Safe-to-Spend). The failure mode is
unchanged: building everything, shipping nothing. Each phase is independently shippable and
killable against §13.

- **Phase 1 — Substrate + Spine.** Events/projections/rebuild tooling (§12); mode variable
  end-to-end (provider, tokens, term catalog, server flag — §10.2); **the glanceable number
  (§5.2) and the first action-moment alerts (§5.1)**. Exit: determinism tests green; slider-Off
  pixel-identical to today; first alert→fix loop live.
  *Why the spine is in phase 1:* it's the highest-evidence, lowest-build-cost value in the whole
  plan — the game must decorate something that already works.
- **Phase 2 — Daily skeleton.** Streak policy + wards + Revival Run; the daily verb; overnight
  tick; template daily beat; basic Grace (ward auto-equip + strain copy). Exit: 60-second daily
  loop end-to-end; E2/E6 instrumented.
- **Phase 3 — Flagship.** `Liability` + closure-framed Boss (phases, defeat-by-date, seal/banish,
  round-up auto-attack) + habit-monsters + seeded ASCII rendering + payoff juice + closure card.
  Exit: E1 instrumented; first real closure celebrated.
- **Phase 4 — Voice.** Chronicler drawer + voice bible + canon file + **Weekly Saga Chapter with
  the one-action rule** + 1/7-LLM daily beat + advice-firewall CI. Exit: 4 consecutive weekly
  chapters; zero red-team leaks.
- **Phase 5 — The novel hook.** Expedition map as Full-mode home (two-homes IA switch), draft,
  forks, fog states, run scoring → prestige, failure-mints-story, monthly trailer. Exit: one full
  expedition with trough-plan content scheduled.
- **Phase 6 — Depth.** Heat **with self-funding sweeps (E9)** + full Grace + bestiary surface +
  homestead weekly/monthly clocks (R2 reveal design) + reflective tags/regret-monster +
  Ascension. Exit: E3/E4/E9 running.
- **Phase 7 — Arc.** Campaigns; Tier-1/2 challenges with graduation/soft-landing exits;
  graduation/fade system (E5); outcomes page v0 (internal).

**MVP = Phases 1–4.** The smallest thing that tests the real bet: *evidence-backed money
mechanics, delivered through a world with a voice, beat the same mechanics in a silent dashboard.*
If Phases 1–4 fail their metrics, v4 stops — and what remains is still a better finance app than
